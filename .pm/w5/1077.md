# Accept native rounding across repeated purchases in Cash Flow

**Major · cli · 45–60m · A1 · worker1.** A second ordinary purchase makes
monthly/yearly Cash Flow refuse a native-valid book. Each purchase succeeds
through real `bea add transaction`, and each reports correctly on its own
day. Together they exit **2**, without a report, saying the transactions
“do not conserve USD at cost”. The native at-cost positions, cash movement,
balance sheet, Holdings and BQL remain valid. Three purchases fail too;
one purchase or two exactly divisible purchases work. No product fix.

Check exits 0. Native cash opens January at **5000 USD** and closes at
**3000 USD**; the six shares have native total cost **2000 USD**. The two
lots have their ordinary, distinct acquisition dates, January 5 and 6.
Each three-share purchase books unit cost

`2000.000000000000000000000000 USD`; it does not retain the sum of two

Both individual days exit 0 and show their true cash boundaries,

Yearly, inclusive bounds, a complete Assets account filter, `--allow-errors`,

No read changes source bytes. The handwritten native reference, actual

## Root cause and bounded repair

[`_cash_flow`](../../cli/src/bea_engine/report.py#L994) accumulates each
price-free transaction's converted residual independently (:1077–1094).
It separately builds per-account inventories and converts their aggregated
lots (:1097), then sums activity rows and sections (:1128–1129).
The new guard compares `moved - net_change` with the exact sum of the
transaction residuals (:1135–1140). Those two calculations perform native
Decimal multiplication/addition at different stages. They need not have the
same rounding residue, even for valid transactions with no prices.

Here, each transaction converts its lot to `999.9999999999999999999999999`
and leaves −1e−25. The combined stock cost rounds natively to 2000, so
`moved = net_change = -2000` and their difference is zero. `rounding` is
−2e−25; exact inequality rejects the correct combined amounts. With three
purchases, the same issue compares zero with −3e−25. This is ordinary
native arithmetic, rather than the first-value-to-zero loss repaired by
[1072](./done/1072.md). Quantities here are the small integers 3, 6 and 9.

`17d104c18` introduced this independent residual sum/comparison for 1071.
Caller search finds one engine dispatch to `_cash_flow` (:137); the
[frontend](../../cli/src/cli/commands/report.py#L620) propagates the refusal
to both human and JSON output. The inventory reduction and conversion use
native booked costs; they are not evidence that either ledger or lot should
be rewritten. This finding is the guard's aggregation policy.

Account for accepted native rounding through the same grouping/conversion
stages that produce the statement, while independently preserving 1068's
unsupported priced-FX rejection. An exact sum of per-transaction residues
is not a sufficient aggregate equality criterion. Keep native precision,
small legitimate values, actual cash boundaries, residual disclosure,
whole-transaction account filtering, date bounds and existing role semantics.
Do not use floats, a blanket epsilon, global precision changes or unconditional
acceptance of `moved - net_change`. CLI-local; no dependency or API changes.
Add meaningful real-CLI regressions and run `make check-all` inside `cli/`
when implementing.

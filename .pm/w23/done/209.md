# 209 — Ledger-backed calls failed with `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` for ~12 minutes starting at a 100 KB `runBqlQuery`; cause unverified, needs a non-production reproduction

**Severity:** unverified — major if one authenticated oversize query can take the ledger service down, none if the outage was unrelated. **Estimate:** 30–60 minutes to reproduce on a local stack. **Owner:** `backend-cluster/ledger` and `backend-cluster/backend-v2`. **Pillar:** A1.

## What was observed (2026-11-01, production, PDT)

- ~01:25 — `runBqlQuery`, `checkLedger` and resource reads on a synthetic ledger answered normally.
- ~01:33 — a probe sent `runBqlQuery` with a 100,155-byte JSON body (`SELECT 1 WHERE 1=1 AND 1=1 AND …`, 12.5k terms). Answer after 1.9 s: `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE: Ledger API is temporarily unavailable`. A 1,000,155-byte body of the same shape answered the same after 5.1 s. 5 MB and 12 MB bodies were refused earlier with the documented `413` / "exceeds the 2 MiB limit".
- From then until ~01:47 every ledger-engine call failed identically: `runBqlQuery` and `checkLedger` over MCP, REST `trial-balance` → `503`, and the public `/ledger/open_ledger/example` page → `500`. Git-backed calls (`listLedgers`, `readLedgerFiles`) kept working. At 01:47 MCP queries recovered; the example page was still `500`.

## What is not known

- Whether the oversize queries caused the outage or it coincided with an unrelated deploy or restart (other workers pushed to `main` at the time). The 100 KB call was the first failure observed, but a failure just before it cannot be ruled out.
- The probe was not repeated on production, and the local stack was not running (`.pm/DO_NOT_DO.md` forbids provisioning a second one for MCP testing).

## Controls

`w23/m5` shipped a POST hop so a 16 KB-URL-encoded query succeeds or is refused `BAD_USER_INPUT` naming a cap. A 100 KB query is neither: it is reported as a dependency outage. A trivial query on another ledger and the example ledger failed identically while the outage lasted.

## Repro to run on a local or staging stack (not production)

1. `runBqlQuery` with `SELECT 1 WHERE ` + `1=1 AND ` × 12 500 + `1=1` (≈ 100 KB), then × 125 000 (≈ 1 MB).
2. Watch the ledger service's logs, memory and restarts; then run `SELECT 1 LIMIT 1` and check whether it recovers on its own and how long that takes.

## Expected

Either the query runs, or it is refused `BAD_USER_INPUT` naming the cap (`w23/m5` DoD), and neither outcome degrades other callers.

## Fix

Pending reproduction. If the ledger service dies on a deeply nested expression, cap the query's size or expression depth at the ledger boundary and return a typed refusal on REST, GraphQL and MCP together.

## Dedupe

Searched `.pm` for `oversize`, `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`, `Ledger API is temporarily unavailable`: `w23/m5` (16 KB URL cliff) is the nearest; `w23/072` is a different `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` (lost write race).

---

**Resolved 2026-11-01** — closed by triage: already fixed by `0dbe35519` (`backend-cluster/ledger`, `backend-v2`). Cause confirmed: a long flat operator chain overflows the engine's WASM stack, the trap left the worker's instance unusable, and every later ledger call routed to it failed until restart. Now `bql-complexity.ts` refuses the note's 100 KB and 1 MB shapes with `BAD_USER_INPUT` (25001 and 250001 operators vs. a limit of 500) before the engine sees them; the worker pool retires a poisoned instance after a failed task; `/healthz` parses through the real pool and 503s when the engine cannot. Verified at HEAD: `bql-complexity` and `healthz` unit tests, `bql-parity` (26), and `yarn verify:rustledger` (live post-trap regression) all pass. Production still needs a deploy of that commit; no repo change is outstanding.

## Objective

Holdings and BQL `value()` price an asset from its `@` sale while the statements and net worth say the price is missing. Follow stock: use the stock price source, and disclose a position that has none.

## Context

- `w1/229`: holdings 90 USD, BQL `value(sum(position))` 90.00 USD, net worth omits the asset; no `price` directives and no `implicit_prices` plugin
- `w23/done/m16` disclosed unpriced report conversions

## Steps

1. Decide the price source from stock behavior and record it
2. Align holdings and BQL `value()`
3. Disclose unpriced positions in the reports

## Result

The contradiction in `w1/229` was holdings, not the reports: the reports already use `price` directives alone, as stock does without `implicit_prices`, while holdings valued through the engine's BQL `value()` and `getprice()`, which also derive a price from every `@` annotation (QAX at 15 USD). Following stock's price source, holdings now asks the engine only for units and cost and reads the `price` directives with `SELECT date, currency, amount FROM prices` (both engines list directives only; checked on the fixtures): `valueHoldingRows` takes the latest directive on or before today for the pair or its inverse, values a lot with no directive at cost and reports it as the existing `valuation: "partial"` missing leg, and quotes the conversion rate the same way. The two shell queries run together. Tests: `holdings.test.ts` (latest on or before the day, the later of two on one day, inverse pair, cost fallback with no quote, conversion leg, an unreadable row), `ledger-finance-service.test.ts` (the detail query asks for no price, the prices query is issued, a lot with no directive is valued at cost), and the oracle's `report-plain-trial-balance-at_value` documents the engine's implied-price behavior. The engine's own BQL `value()` is not changed: it is an upstream behavior, recorded in the BQL allowlist with `w1/229`.

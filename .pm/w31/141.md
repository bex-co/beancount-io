# Holdings values a currency in itself through the operating currency: 212 EUR held without a cost reads `211.999999999999999999152 EUR` at a price of `0.999999999999999999996 EUR` under `conversion=EUR`, and 50 GBP under `conversion=GBP` reads `valuation: "partial"` with a missing GBP→USD quote, on MCP, REST and GraphQL (`bea report holdings` refuses the GBP case)

**Minor · backend-v2 (`src/features/ledger/utils/holdings.ts`) and cli (`src/bea_engine/report.py`) · 30–45m · A1.** A position held without a cost in a currency that is not money in the ledger (not an operating or cost currency) is a holding. When the report is converted into that same currency, the position is already in it: 212 EUR is worth 212 EUR, at a price of 1. The hosted holdings report drops the requested currency from the currencies it values the position into, values it in the operating currency first, and converts back. The way back uses an inverse rate rounded to 20 decimal places, so 212 EUR comes out as `211.999999999999999999152 EUR`. When the commodity has no quote in the operating currency, the report says that quote is missing and marks the valuation `partial`, though no quote is needed. The balance sheet, `bean-query` and `bea report holdings -x EUR` all answer 212 EUR. `docs/mcp.md` says "Holdings agree with `bea report holdings`". The CLI has the second half of the bug too: `bea report holdings -x GBP` refuses the report.

Found by `/loopx w31` routine `mcp-qa` (`/qa-find-bugs-mcp w31 LAND=1`) on 2026-11-27. Target: `https://beancount.io` (`beancount-mcp 1.0.0`, protocol `2025-11-25`). Credential: an unpinned read/write `bcio_` key on the QA account. Writes went only to the designated QA ledger `<qa-owner>/mcp-agent-eval`, with accounts and payees prefixed `qa-20261127`: probe commits `69bf0943` and `4ee66b53`, restore commit `ce2062f4`. `main.bean` was restored byte for byte afterwards (git blob `f1a0b83c`, sha256 `77c3a591efd311e0`) with a clean check. ## Root cause

- backend-v2 `composeHoldings`, the loop over uncosted positions (`holdings.ts:621`–`689`): the candidate currencies `[conversionTarget, ...operating, ...quoted]` are filtered with `currency !== commodity` (`:649`), so the requested currency is dropped when it is the commodity. `held` becomes USD, and the market value is `units × quote(EUR, USD)`. That is then converted back with `quote(USD, EUR)` (`:664`), the inverse that `priceLookup` computes as `new BigNumber(1).dividedBy(rate)` (`:208`), which BigNumber rounds to 20 decimal places (`0.83333333333333333333`). With no quote in any candidate, `miss(commodity, candidates[0], true)` (`:657`) names a GBP→USD leg that this report never needed.
- `priceLookup` has no identity pair, so the costed path's direct quote (`valueHoldingRows`, `quote(commodity, conversionTarget)` at `:270`) also misses for a lot whose commodity is the conversion currency. That case was not exercised live.
- cli `_holdings` (`report.py:871`–`880`) filters its candidates the same way. The EUR case still comes out right, because Beancount's price map answers 1 for a currency in itself, and `_value_holding` takes that as the direct quote. The GBP case has no candidate with a quote, so `(GBP, USD)` lands in `in_units` and the report is refused.

## Fix

1. backend-v2: a position whose commodity is the conversion currency is already in it. Value it at its units, with price 1 in that currency, and record no missing leg. Do this in the uncosted branch of `composeHoldings`, and make `priceLookup` answer rate 1 when base and quote are the same, so the costed path agrees. One change covers MCP, REST and GraphQL, which share `holdings.ts`.
2. cli: in `_holdings`' uncosted branch, value a position whose commodity is `target` at 1 in the target before searching other currencies, so `bea report holdings -x GBP` answers 50.00 GBP with `valuation: complete`.

## Adjacent, out of scope

The same 20-decimal-place inverse rounds every cross-currency holding slightly differently from Beancount's 28-significant-digit `Decimal`. 11 VTI quoted at 125 USD, under `conversion=EUR` with EUR at 1.20 USD, reads price `104.16666666666666666625 EUR` and `market_value` `1145.83333333333333332875 EUR`. Beancount answers `104.1666666666666666666666667`.

## Objective

Decide, per verb, whether an agent should reach it at all — and if so, under which scope. Nine of the fifteen are `admin`-class, and the family reaches a third party holding the customer's financial data. This is the decision the port depends on, not a detail inside it.

## Context

- The sharp ones: `unlinkPlaidItem` severs a bank connection; `syncPlaidTransactions` calls Plaid on the customer's behalf and may be rate-limited or billed; `submitPlaidTransactionsToLedger` writes real transactions; `deletePlaidTransactions` removes them.
- The scope vocabulary is closed at `ledger.read` / `ledger.write` / `ledger.admin` and cumulative — `admin` implies `write` implies `read` (ADR 0006 D3). So filing a verb under `admin` does not *restrict* it to careful clients; it means anyone holding `ledger.admin` gets it.
- **REST and MCP need not get the same answer.** A REST caller is a person's script; an MCP caller is a model choosing among options. Leaving a verb REST-only is a legitimate outcome — but per ADR 0008 D1 it must then carry a `structural:` or `deferred:` reason that says which, not a vague one.
- The mapping/suggestion verbs (`suggestPlaidAccountMapping`, `suggestPlaidTransactionCategories`) are advisory reads and the obvious safe starting point.

## Steps

1. Classify each of the fifteen: both surfaces, REST-only, or neither.
2. For anything an agent may write, decide whether it needs a confirmation shape — `dry_run` like `editLedgerFiles`, or a preview verb before a commit verb.
3. Record the decision in ADR 0008 with the reasoning, since it is the first time the surfaces deliberately diverge on a family.

## Out of scope

- The three Link-ceremony verbs — permanently out per ADR 0008 D4.
- Widening the scope vocabulary; that is ADR 0006 D3's decision.

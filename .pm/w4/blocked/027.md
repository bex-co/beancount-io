# "Net Worth" is a cost basis, and nothing on screen says so — $10,823 of market value is absent

**Folded into [w4/m11](./m11/README.md) on 2026-09-15**, promoted from [w4/016](./016.md). Decision taken: option 1 — keep cost basis and label it wherever a converted total appears; option 3 (offer cost, market value, and units) is recorded as [w4/068](./068.md). The milestone is the sole implementation queue; this note retains the original evidence and is dropped when m11 closes.

**Severity:** minor, and framed as a **decision the board should make** rather than a bug: the arithmetic is exactly right and follows Beancount's own default. The gap is that a card labelled *Net Worth* with a trend line reads as market value to anyone who is not an accountant, and the app offers no label and no toggle. **Estimate:** 20–30 minutes to decide and label; longer if a basis toggle is wanted. Owner: mobile (plus a product call). A2: a newcomer evaluating the app against their brokerage app will see a number 10% below what every other tool shows them, with nothing explaining the difference.

Environment: branch `main`, HEAD `038faeeb`, native development build `io.beancount.ios` on iPhone 17e / iOS 26.5, authenticated production session at `https://beancount.io`, own ledger `puncsky/example`. Report-only QA; no ledger mutation. All BQL below is read-only through the MCP credential pinned to that ledger.

| Liabilities | `-$8,123.35` |

Home's Net Worth card reads **`$106,723.05`** — exactly `114,846.40 − 8,123.35`, so the two screens agree.

Three BQL aggregations over the same ledger:

| basis | Assets |
| --- | --- |
| `sum(position)` (raw units) | `USD 8,926.93` + `VBMPX 193.442`, `RGAGX 597.748`, `VEA 36`, `ITOT 104`, `VHT 64`, `GLD 17`, `VACHR -8` |
| `sum(cost(position))` | **`USD 114,846.39944`** ← matches the app to the cent |
| `sum(value(position))` | `USD 125,669.83828` |

So every figure on both screens is `cost(position)`, confirmed exactly. At the ledger's own latest prices, net worth would be **`$117,546.49`** — **$10,823.44 higher, 10.1% of the figure shown.**

The valuation data is present and not sparse: `#prices` carries **141 points each** for `GLD`, `ITOT`, `RGAGX`, `VBMPX`, `VEA` and `VHT`, spanning 2015-01-02 to 2017-09-08 — the ledger's full range.

## Why this is a question and not a defect

Cost basis is Beancount's default for a balance sheet; `value()` is the opt-in, and Fava exposes it as an explicit *At Cost / At Market* toggle. Beancount also offers an `unrealized` option that books the difference into its own account — the schema already carries it (`schema.graphql:406`, `unrealized: String!`), the mobile app never reads it, and `puncsky/example` does not use it (no `Unrealized` or `Conversions` account exists in the ledger).

So the app is following convention, not miscalculating. **I could not establish intent** beyond that, and reasonable finance apps differ on this, which is why this is filed as minor and as a decision.

What tips it into being worth deciding: the label. *Balance* in an accounts tree implies a basis the reader chooses; **Net Worth**, rendered as a headline figure over a trend chart with a percentage change, is personal-finance framing where market value is the near-universal convention. Nothing on either screen says which basis is in use.

## Fix — pick one, then say it on screen

1. **Keep cost basis and label it.** Cheapest and fully defensible. The Accounts column header or the Net Worth card states the basis, so `$106,723.05` cannot be misread as today's market value. This folds into `w4/016`'s existing ask to "mark converted figures as valuations wherever they appear".
2. **Value at market.** Matches the Net Worth framing and makes the price data earn its keep, but changes every total in the app and needs a stale-price story — this ledger's prices stop in 2017, so a 2026 valuation would be quoting nine-year-old marks, which must be disclosed rather than presented as current.
3. **Offer both**, as Fava does. Most work; only worth it if the basis question is expected to recur.

Whichever is chosen, do not change one screen without the other — Accounts and Home agree today, and that consistency is worth keeping.

**Note for whoever picks this up:** `w4/016` says "Do not change the Accounts tree's operating-currency **totals**, which are correct and reconcile exactly." That instruction is right *within that note's scope* — it is guarding against breaking arithmetic that verifiably reconciles, not asserting that cost basis is the settled product answer. This note is the place that question lives; the two do not conflict.

**Acceptance:** the basis is stated wherever a converted total appears (Accounts tree and the Home Net Worth card at minimum); if option 2 or 3 is taken, `puncsky/example` reconciles against `sum(value(position))` to the cent and a price older than some agreed threshold is disclosed as stale. Add coverage for a holding with prices, one without (`VACHR` has none), and a pure-USD control.

## Blast radius

Every total the app renders for a ledger holding priced commodities — the Accounts tree at every level, Home's Net Worth card and its chart, and Reports. Reproduced on `puncsky/example`. Not exercised: `open_ledger/crypto-example` (which holds STETH/BTC and would show a far larger gap), `open_ledger/minimax` (MUSD throughout, no conversion, so unaffected), and whether the server or the client computes the figure — `w4/016` leaves that open and it must be settled before implementing, since it decides whether this is mobile-only or also backend.

## Adjacent states

Everything checked in this pass reconciles exactly and should not be disturbed: Assets `$114,846.40` = `US $109,529.34` + `Bank:Mercury:Checking $5,317.06`; Liabilities `-$8,123.35` = `US:Chase:Slate -$8,123.35` + `AccountsPayable $0.00`; and Home's net worth equals Assets + Liabilities to the cent. The Income and Expenses USD components match `cost(position)` exactly too.

Confirming a detail `w4/016` raised: `VACHR` and `IRAUSD` are omitted from every total because they carry **no price and no cost currency** — verified here, since `#prices` holds entries for exactly the six priced securities and nothing for those two. Their omission is therefore mechanical rather than arbitrary, which supports `w4/016`'s fix (disclose the omission) over any attempt to force them into a dollar figure.

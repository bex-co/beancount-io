## Objective

Prove relationship changes and destructive control-plane workflows preserve current-source, failure, rate-budget, audit, and side-effect-ordering behavior.

## Context

- Cross-request caches would let revoked collaborators retain stale authority.

## Steps

1. Exercise downgrade/delete/leave/public-private/rename/delete lifecycles and exact client-visible denial/not-found behavior.
2. Confirm every call reads current source facts; inject source failure and require error-level log, `error` audit, service unavailable, and no cleanup/domain mutation.
3. Verify destructive rate limits do not loosen, duplicate calls audit independently, concurrent operation IDs stay isolated, and atomic mutation predicates remain.

## Files

- ledger authorization/workflow integration tests and helpers

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Revocation/downgrade is effective on the next request.
- [ ] Failed authorization performs no partial cleanup or mutation.
- [ ] No cross-request permission cache or relationship copy exists.
- [ ] No request-local authorization decision memo suppresses a check/audit, and source outage is not counted as policy denial.

## Out of scope

- Distributed tuple consistency because no tuple store exists.

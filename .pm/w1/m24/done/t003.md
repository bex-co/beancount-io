## Objective

The no-code CSV path rejects currency symbols, thousands separators, and accounting parentheses with a bare `cannot parse amount` and no recovery hint — which covers most US exports — while accepting `NaN` and `Infinity` as ready rows that then fail at apply time with `Invalid token`.

## Context

- `w3/256`: `$4.50`, `1,000.00`, and `(4.50)` are all rejected.
- `w3/265`: `NaN` / `Infinity` amounts are marked ready in the preview; `--apply` then writes tokens Beancount rejects.
- Comma-decimal amounts (`1.000,00`) accompany the `;` delimiter from t001.
- Amount parsing sits in `cli/src/bea_engine/csv_mapper.py`; exactness rules for written numbers are m27's subject.

## Steps

1. Accept a leading or trailing currency symbol, grouped thousands, accounting parentheses and trailing-minus negatives, and comma decimals, resolving the separator ambiguity from the whole column rather than one cell.
2. Preserve exact decimal text — parse to `Decimal`, never through a float.
3. Reject `NaN`, `Infinity` and other non-finite spellings at preview time with the row named, so `--apply` can never write them.
4. When parsing fails, show the offending cell and the spellings that are accepted.

## Files

- cli/src/bea_engine/csv_mapper.py
- cli/src/bea_engine/importing.py
- cli/tests/test_import.py

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `$1,000.00`, `(4.50)`, `4.50-`, and `1.000,00` (with a comma-decimal column) all import to the exact decimal value.
- [ ] A column mixing `1,000.00` and `1.000,00` is refused rather than guessed.
- [ ] `NaN` / `Infinity` rows are blocked in the preview and `--apply` is refused; no invalid token reaches the ledger.
- [ ] A failed parse names the cell, the row, and the accepted spellings.

## Out of scope

- Currency inference from the symbol — the account still supplies the commodity.
- Exactness on the write side generally, which m27 owns.

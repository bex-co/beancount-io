# `bea report capital-gains` calls a lot held exactly one year "more than one year" when the year spans 29 February

Found by `/qa-find-bugs-cli w1` on 2026-11-15 (HEAD `9eb92d31b`, `bea 0.3.1`, macOS 26.5, checkout `uv run bea`, stdin closed, isolated `BEA_CONFIG_DIR`/`XDG_CACHE_HOME`, offline synthetic ledger). Severity: minor. A1: an agent preparing a realized-gains summary reads `long_held` and the `more_than_one_year` total from `--json`, and the answer depends on whether a leap day fell inside the holding period.

**Contract.** `cli/docs/USAGE.md` (Reports, `capital-gains`): rows "bucket into held one year or less vs more than one year". The human totals read `One year or less` / `More than one year`, and CSV writes `term` as `short`/`long`. The doc also calls this "a bare day count with no tax rules attached". A bare count of days still has to match the label, though. A lot bought on 2023-03-01 and sold on 2024-03-01 was held exactly one year, not more than one.

Actual (`check` is clean, exit 0, stderr empty, file unchanged):

```
SOLD        ...  ACQUIRED    HELD              ...  GAIN
2023-03-01  ...  2022-03-01  365 days (short)  ...  10 USD
2024-03-01  ...  2023-03-01  366 days (long)   ...  20 USD

  One year or less:    10 USD
  More than one year:  20 USD
```

Both lots were held for the same calendar span (one year to the day), but the leap-year lot lands in the other bucket. CSV says `term` `short` and `long`, and `--json` says `long_held: false` and `true`.

Expected: both rows are `one year or less` / `short` / `long_held: false`. A lot is "more than one year" only when the sale date is after the acquisition date plus one calendar year: `sold > add_one_year(acquired)`, where 29 February plus a year is 28 February (or 1 March, if you prefer; whichever convention you pick, document it). `held_days` stays the plain day count.

**Root cause.** `cli/src/bea_engine/report.py:1051` (`"long_held": (held > 365)`) and the bucket at line 1061 (`row["held_days"] > 365`) compare a day count with 365. A year that contains 29 February has 366 days, so a holding of exactly one year passes `> 365`.

**Blast radius.** The hosted report repeats the rule: `backend-cluster/backend-v2/src/features/ledger/utils/gains.ts:126` (bucket) and `:200` (`long_held`). It serves the capital-gains report on REST, GraphQL and MCP, so the fix must land on all three surfaces in the same change (root `AGENTS.md` parity rule). The customer skill `skills/beancount-trade/references/realized-gains.md:56` has a BQL fallback, `(date - cost_date) > 365 AS long_held`, with the same flaw. `cli/src/cli/report_csv.py:220` and `cli/src/cli/commands/report.py:771` only render `long_held` and are fixed once the producer is right. Control: holdings that do not span 29 February are bucketed correctly.

**Fix** (about 45 minutes; `cli`, plus `backend-cluster/backend-v2` and the skill reference). Compute `long_held` by comparing calendar dates, so the sale must come after the acquisition date plus one year, and bucket the totals by `long_held` rather than by recomputing from `held_days`. Mirror this in `gains.ts`. Change the skill's BQL to compare dates (for example, compare `date` with `cost_date` shifted by a year, if beanquery can express it), or say in the reference that its day-count fallback can mis-bucket a lot across a leap day. Add one sentence to USAGE.md's `capital-gains` paragraph defining "more than one year" by calendar date. Regression tests: the two lots above in a CLI test (human totals, CSV `term`, JSON `long_held`), and the same pair in the backend gains unit test. Revert the fix and check that the tests fail.

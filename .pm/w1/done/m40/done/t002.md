## Objective

A subscription has at most one pending change:

- `subscriptionStatus` exposes `scheduledChange { priceId, effectiveAt }`.
- A new change replaces a scheduled one.
- Any change withdraws a pending cancellation, and cancelling releases a scheduled change.
- `cancelScheduledPlanChange` releases the schedule and leaves the current plan untouched.
- The preview says which pending change a new choice replaces.

## Context

- Cancellation today sets `cancel_at_period_end` (`stripe-service.ts`), and `resumeSubscription` clears it.
- PRFAQ005 question 14 (last two rows) and question 16.
- Billing operations are GraphQL-only and browser-session-only, with parity, op-class and rate-limit entries (see m39/t001).

## Steps

1. Add `scheduledChange` to status, and the `replacesScheduledChange` and `withdrawsCancellation` flags to the preview.
2. Make change, cancel and resume mutually exclusive in the service layer, replacing or releasing the other pending change explicitly.
3. Add `cancelScheduledPlanChange` with its authorization action and gates, and update the dashboard schema copy.

## Files

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each combination ends with exactly one pending change, visible in status and in the preview: scheduled plus a new change, cancellation plus a change, and a scheduled change plus cancellation.
- [ ] Releasing a schedule leaves the current subscription and price unchanged.
- [ ] Gates pass, and the operation has no REST or MCP eligibility.

## Implementation notes

- **Status and preview**:
  - `subscriptionStatus` reports each subscription's `scheduledChange { priceId, effectiveAt }`: the first phase after the current one that moves the subscription to another price. A schedule in its last phase, where the change already took effect, reports nothing pending.
  - Only the full Billing view lists subscriptions with the schedule expanded (`expandSchedule`). Tier checks, webhooks and the active-only status skip it. `pendingChange` throws on an unexpanded schedule, so a forgotten expansion cannot read as "nothing pending".
  - `previewPlanChange` adds `replacesScheduledChange` (the pending change a different choice replaces; null when re-choosing the same target) and `withdrawsCancellation`. Both are empty for a refused choice.
- **One pending change at a time**:
  - A scheduled change replaces the schedule's next phase (t001).
  - An immediate change releases the schedule and sends `cancel_at_period_end: false` in the same update as the price change.
    - Test mode with declined and bank-approval cards showed that Stripe holds the flag in the pending update until the payment succeeds, so a failed change keeps its cancellation.
    - The flag is always sent, keeping the request body stable for the idempotency key.
  - A scheduled change withdraws a pending cancellation through `end_behavior: release` (t001's probe).
  - Cancelling releases the schedule (t001).
  - `resumeSubscription` needs nothing: our flows never leave a cancellation and a schedule together.
- **`cancelScheduledPlanChange(clientId)`** (`cancelScheduledChange` in `operations/plan-change-schedule.ts`) releases the schedule. The current price, discounts and period are untouched.
  - Nothing pending is a success, so a repeated undo is harmless.
  - Two active subscriptions are refused rather than guessed between.
  - It is a GraphQL-only, browser-session billing write: action `user.billing.plan_change.cancel` (write-billing), with an op-class row, a session-only rate budget, the authz README row, and GraphQL mutation coverage at 66.
- The dashboard schema copy and the generated types gain the new fields; the UI that uses them is t007 and t008.

## Objective

Premium yearly subscriptions and switches started from the dashboard use the $143.88 price. Subscribers on the $99.99 yearly or $9.99 monthly Premium prices still resolve to Premium and see their price labelled as legacy. The pricing page keeps selling exactly what it sells today until it switches to $143.88, outside this repository.

## Context

- **Pricing-page compatibility** (milestone README): the pricing page creates Checkout with `createSubscriptionSession` for the $99.99 Premium yearly price. Refusing that price would break Premium yearly checkout on `beancount.io/pricing`.
- Backend `SUBSCRIPTION_CONFIG` (`backend-cluster/backend-v2/src/features/stripe/service/stripe.ts`) lists every recognized price. Dashboard `dashboard/src/features/user-settings/pages/general/stripe-config.ts` holds one monthly and one yearly price per tier, and `dashboard/src/common/lib/subscription/tier-config.ts` hard-codes monthly price strings.
- PRFAQ005 question 21 decision 3 and question 11.

## Steps

1. Add the new price to `SUBSCRIPTION_CONFIG` as Premium yearly. Mark the $99.99 and $9.99 prices as legacy:
   - The dashboard never offers a legacy price.
   - `changePlan` refuses one as a target.
   - Tier resolution still recognizes both.
   - `createSubscriptionSession` keeps accepting the $99.99 price.
2. In the dashboard, point Premium yearly at the new price. Keep one checked-in table of list amounts next to the price IDs, replacing the hard-coded strings.
3. Expose whether the current price is legacy, so the summary card can label it.
4. Add an inbox note on this workstream: retire the $99.99 price from `createSubscriptionSession` once the pricing page sells $143.88.

- [x] A dashboard Premium yearly preview or Checkout uses the new price, and `changePlan` refuses a legacy target.

- [x] A test pins the dashboard price table to the backend configuration. It checks that every yearly per-month figure is the yearly amount ÷ 12, rounded to cents, and that every tier saves 20%.

## Out of scope

- Moving existing subscribers to the new price, and changing the pricing page.

## Implementation notes

- **Backend** (`SUBSCRIPTION_CONFIG`):
  - The $143.88 price (`price_1US5htEqsEqs2tLVENCzLaZB`, from t004) is Premium yearly.
  - The $99.99 yearly price is now `retired`, like the $9.99 monthly. A retired price still resolves to PREMIUM and still renews, but `resolveTargetPlan` refuses it as a change target, and the split alternative's same-tier lookup skips it.
  - `createSubscriptionSession` checks only that a price is configured, so the pricing page's $99.99 Checkout keeps working. Follow-up [w1/176](../../../done/176.md) retires that path once the page sells $143.88.
  - A config test requires exactly one offered price per tier and cycle, so every change has one target.
- **Portal pinned in code.** The billing-only configuration from t004 (`bpc_1US5i0EqsEqs2tLVVF907WfA`) is a literal beside the price IDs. Every portal session passes it.
  - This supersedes t003 part 1's `STRIPE_PORTAL_CONFIGURATION_ID`. The variable, its warning and its wiring in the four `deploy/*` Compose files and env examples are removed, because nobody had to set it in production.
- **Dashboard:**
  - `stripePlanConfig.premium.yearly` is the new price at 14388 cents.
  - The $99.99 price joined the legacy table, so the summary card labels it through the existing `isLegacyPrice`.
  - `planForPrice` falls back to the legacy table. A change scheduled onto $99.99 before this deploy therefore keeps its plan label and its keep-current analytics event.
  - The current plan is still derived from tier plus interval, so a $99.99 subscriber reads as Premium · Yearly.
  - `TierInfo.price`'s hard-coded strings are gone. The AI CFO upgrade panel formats the amounts table in the app locale.
- **Parity test** (`stripe-config.test.ts`) reads the backend's plans from its source in the monorepo and checks:
  - every dashboard tier and cycle is an offered backend price of that tier and interval;
  - every offered backend price is in the dashboard;
  - every retired one is in `LEGACY_PRICE_IDS`;
  - each yearly per-month figure is the yearly amount ÷ 12 and saves 20%.
  It reads exactly one `plans` array, with comments stripped. A mutation check pointing Premium yearly back at $99.99 failed three of its cases.
  The dashboard CI workflow also runs when the backend's `stripe.ts` changes, so a backend-only price change cannot pass while breaking parity.
- **Known transition:** until the pricing page sells $143.88, a visitor who buys Premium yearly there gets $99.99 and sees the legacy label. That ends once the pricing page switches, which needs this revision deployed first ([w1/176](../../../done/176.md)).

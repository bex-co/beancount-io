# Percent signs are written two ways within one locale ("38 %" and "73%"), and loading and saving strings use three ASCII dots

**Severity:** low and cosmetic (mostly VoiceOver text). **Estimate:** 20 minutes. Owner: mobile.

**Findings (A3).** `tsx` scans over all 12 locales plus English, run through the app's real `I18n` instance:
- **Percent spacing.** `breakdownRowShareSpending`, `breakdownRowShareEarnings` and `receiptUploadingProgress` write `{{percent}} %` in de, fr and sk, while `budgetPanelRowValue` and `budgetPanelRowValueIncome` write `{{percent}}%`. fa mixes `٪` with `%`. French reads "… 38 % des dépenses" on a breakdown row and "… 73% utilisé" on a budget row. The spaces are plain U+0020, not no-break.
- **Ellipsis.** `loading` is lowercase with three ASCII dots in every locale ("loading...", "laden...", "cargando..."); it shows as the sign-in message on the `oauth/callback` screen. `saving`, `deleting`, `pleaseInput`, `openAccountSaving`, `loadingMore` and `ledgerEditorSaving` also use `...` instead of `…`.
- **Full stop.** `createLedgerAmountInvalid` ends with a period in English but not in the other locales.

Where each shows: the Reports breakdown and Home budget row spoken values, the receipt upload progress text (the one visible case), and the sign-in callback message.

Not run natively: VoiceOver can't be driven here, and the receipt upload would use AI quota. **Native steps:** Settings → Language → Français, then read the receipt upload text "Envoi de la photo… N %"; restore English afterwards.

## Fix

- Pick one percent convention per locale (a no-break space for de, fr and sk). Better, format the percent with `Intl.NumberFormat(locale, { style: "percent" })` and make the placeholder `{{percent}}`.
- Use `…` in the loading and saving strings, and settle the full-stop convention for `createLedgerAmountInvalid`.

**Acceptance:** for each locale, every `{{percent}}` occurrence is followed by the same separator, and no UI string ends in three ASCII dots.

## Dedupe

The `.pm` hits (w21/788, 790 and 965) are about what the number means, not the glyph spacing. Searched for "percent", "12 %" and "NBSP".
